# Rule checks for New Zealand and Australian companies

Checked 29 October 2026. Run `npm run cap -- compliance` (or `/compliance`). Each company's `jurisdiction` (NZ or AU) and `company_type` say which rules apply. These checks find records that look inconsistent with the rules below. They check what is recorded in this database, not what was lodged with a regulator, and they are not legal or tax advice. Read the source before relying on a check, and confirm with your lawyer or accountant when it matters.

| Check | What the data shows | Rule and source | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| nz-share-issue-notice | A New Zealand company issued shares (an issue, an option exercise or a conversion) and no notice is recorded within 10 working days. | Companies Act 1993 s43: the board sends the Registrar notice of a share issue within 10 working days of issue. The Companies Office: "You must file notice of a share issue with us within 10 working days." [Companies Office guidance](https://companies-register.companiesoffice.govt.nz/help-centre/shares-and-shareholders/issuing-shares-in-a-company/), [Companies Act 1993](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1993/0105/latest/DLM319570.html) | Working days here skip weekends but not public holidays, so a due date near a holiday is a day or two early. |
| au-share-change-notice | An Australian company issued or cancelled shares, or an Australian proprietary company changed its members register (a transfer), and no Form 484 is recorded within 28 days. | Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) s254X (notice of a share issue within 28 days) and s254Y (cancellations); proprietary companies notify changes to members within 28 days (s178A). ASIC: "Notifications must be received within 28 days of the change occurring." [ASIC: changes to share structure](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/1192244/ra-user-guide-change-company-details-change-to-share-structure.pdf), [ASIC: changes to members register](https://asic.gov.au/online-services/registered-agents/registered-agents-help/how-to-change-company-details-change-to-members-register) | A proprietary company reports changes to its top 20 members per class; the check flags every transfer. Late fees and Form 484 details are ASIC's. |
| au-ess-statement | A financial year (to 30 June) in which an Australian company granted options or shares to Australian-resident employees, with no ESS statement recorded by 14 July. | Taxation Administration Act 1953 Sch 1 Subdiv 392: employers give each employee an ESS statement by 14 July after the end of the year. [ATO: reporting requirements for employers](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/employee-share-schemes/in-detail/employer-reporting-requirements/reporting-requirements-for-employers) | Treats every grant to an Australian-resident employee as reportable. Which interests your plan must report depends on how it is taxed: confirm with your tax adviser, then record the filing to clear it. A due date on a weekend moves to the next business day. |
| au-ess-report | The same year, with no ESS annual report to the ATO recorded by 14 August. | Same source: the ESS annual report is lodged electronically by 14 August after the end of each financial year. | As above. Taxing points on exercise in later years are not tracked separately. |
| nz-ess-income | A New Zealand-resident holder exercised options under a plan not marked `nz-exempt`, and no employment income information is recorded by 2 working days after the deferral date (20 days after the share scheme taxing date). | Tax Administration Act 1994 s23K; Inland Revenue: treat the ESS deferral date (20 days after the taxing date) as the payday and file within 2 working days if filing electronically. [Inland Revenue: filing employment information about ESS benefits](https://www.ird.govt.nz/employing-staff/paying-staff/employee-share-schemes/filing-ei-about-ess-benefits) | Takes the exercise date as the taxing date and estimates the benefit from the latest valuation on file. The taxing date can differ (for example shares held under restrictions), and former employees' share benefits with no tax withheld need not be reported. |
| au-50-non-employee | An Australian proprietary company has more than 45 (near limit) or more than 50 (breached) shareholders who are not employees. | Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) s113: a proprietary company has no more than 50 non-employee shareholders; joint holders of a parcel count as one; employee shareholders do not count. [s113](https://www5.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ca2001172/s113.html) | Each holder record counts as one. Joint holdings and crowd-sourced funding shareholders are not modelled. Option holders are not shareholders until they exercise. |
| register-address | A shareholder (or, in Australia, an option holder) has no address on file. | NZ: Companies Act 1993 s87 (the share register records the names and last known addresses of shareholders). AU: Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) s169 (register of members) and s170 (register of option holders) require names and addresses. | Checks that an address is recorded, not that it is current. |
| option-register-details | An Australian company has outstanding options with no expiry date recorded. | Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) s170: the register of option holders records, among other things, the period during which the options can be exercised. | The full list of s170 details is in the Act. This check looks only for the exercise period. |
| board-approval | A share issue, conversion, cancellation, buyback or option grant has no board approval date. | Operator policy in this base: every change to the register cites the board resolution that approved it. NZ: Companies Act 1993 s47 requires a board resolution before shares are issued. | Records the date only. Keep the signed minutes or resolution with your company records. |
| pool-overgranted | More options are outstanding or exercised than the plan's pool allows. | Operator policy: grants stay inside the pool the shareholders approved. | `grant-options` refuses to overgrant. This catches imported or hand-edited data. |
| convertible-matured | A SAFE or convertible note is past its maturity date and still outstanding. | Operator policy: a matured note is repaid, converted or extended in writing before the next board meeting. | The terms of each note decide what happens at maturity. |
| ledger-negative | A holder's shares in a class add to less than zero. | Data integrity: the ledger never takes out more than went in. | |
| transfer-unbalanced | The two sides of a transfer do not add to zero. | Data integrity. | |

Change a policy check, or add a rule your company lives under (a shareholders' agreement drag-along threshold, a pre-emptive rights notice period), with `/customise`: it writes a migration and adds the check to `cap.compliance_findings`.
